Stroke: screen and time it

LAMS and RACE flag large vessel occlusion (LVO). No screen diagnoses stroke or tells clot from bleed; adult scales do poorly in children.
ToolChecksPositive
Cincinnati (CPSS)Face droop, arm drift, speechAny 1 abnormal
BE-FASTFAST plus balance and eyes (vision)Any new deficit
LAMS (0-5)Face 0-1, arm drift 0-2, grip 0-2≥4: LVO likely
RACE (0-9)Face, arm, leg, gaze, aphasia or agnosia≥5: LVO likely
  • LVO clues: gaze deviation, aphasia, neglect, dense one-sided weakness
  • Bleed clues (none specific): severe headache, vomiting, falling LOC, very high BP
  • Last known well (LKW): last time seen normal (and by whom), not when found or woke up
  • Glucose on every suspected stroke; treat if <60 mg/dL, then rescreen
  • Mimics: hypoglycemia, seizure with postictal weakness, sepsis, migraine, intoxication
  • Ask: anticoagulants, recent trauma, surgery, bleeding, seizure
Exam tip Exams test LKW: in bed at 10 PM, wakes with deficits at 8 AM, so LKW is 10 PM (or 3 AM if seen normal then). Treatment windows run from LKW.

Stroke: destination and field care

Any stroke center beats a non-stroke hospital. Some patients qualify later with advanced imaging.
From LKWTreatmentEMS destination
≤4.5 h, no LVO signsIV thrombolysisClosest stroke-capable hospital
≤24 h, LVO likelyThrombectomy, plus lytic if eligibleThrombectomy-capable center (TSC or CSC)
  • LVO signs: direct to a TSC per plan, if the extra time will not cost lytic eligibility
  • Going to a distant (45-60 min) TSC did not beat a well-run local stroke center
  • Stroke alert early: LKW, scale and score, glucose, anticoagulants, ETA
  • Scene time ≤15 min; IV line, but avoid multiple attempts
  • Oxygen only if hypoxemic (SpO₂ 94-98%); NPO until a swallow screen
  • Head of stretcher 15-30° if SBP >100, neck neutral; protect weak limbs
  • Cardiac monitor; 12-lead if it does not delay transport
Safety Do not treat high BP in the field: an SBP target of 130-140 did not help, and nitro patches may harm. Correct hypotension. No aspirin until imaging rules out bleeding. Hospitals lower BP to <185/110 before lytics.

STEMI: 12-lead and destination

  • 12-lead within 10 min of first medical contact (FMC); transmit or read it
  • Nondiagnostic with ongoing symptoms: repeat 12-leads (some occlusions lack ST elevation)
  • STEMI: new J-point ST elevation in 2 contiguous leads, ≥1 mm (V2-V3: ≥2 mm men 40+, ≥2.5 mm men <40, ≥1.5 mm women), without LBBB or LVH
  • Inferior STEMI: add right-sided leads (V4R) to look for RV infarct
  • ST depression in V1-V3: think posterior MI; check V7-V9 for ST elevation
  • Early STEMI alert, short scene time, pads on high-risk patients
SituationGoal (ACC/AHA 2025)
Direct to PCI centerFMC to device ≤90 min
Transfer for PCIFMC to device ≤120 min
PCI not possible within 120 min, onset <12 hLytic within 30 min of arrival if no contraindication, then transfer
Cardiogenic shockEmergency PCI or CABG, any time from onset
Exam tip Remember 10-90-120: 12-lead by 10 min, device by 90, or by 120 with a transfer. Bypass non-PCI hospitals when the plan allows.

Aspirin, nitro and oxygen

Typical adult doses for exam study. Follow your agency's protocols.

DrugTypical doseHold if
Aspirin162-325 mg chewed, not enteric-coatedTrue allergy, active bleeding, possible stroke
Nitroglycerin0.4 mg SL every 5 min, up to 3SBP <90-100, PDE5 inhibitor, possible stroke, RV infarct (per protocol)
OxygenIf SpO₂ <90%, dyspnea or heart failureNot routine if SpO₂ ≥90%
Safety No nitrates within 24 h of sildenafil or vardenafil, 48 h of tadalafil, or 12 h of avanafil (ACC/AHA 2025); NASEMSO and many protocols use 48 h for any PDE5 inhibitor. Pulmonary hypertension drugs count too: daily Revatio or Adcirca, riociguat, IV epoprostenol or treprostinil.
Safety Inferior STEMI with hypotension, JVD and clear lungs, or ST elevation in V4R: suspect RV infarct (preload dependent). Classic exam answer: no nitro, cautious fluid bolus. NASEMSO allows nitro with close monitoring; follow your protocol.
